# SBF history and references

## The history of the SBF method

This is a short, selective history of the SBF method, drawn from the
papers listed below. It is not a complete review; see Blakeslee, Ajhar &
Tonry (1999) and Cantiello & Blakeslee (2023, 2024) for that.

**The idea (1988).** John Tonry and Donald Schneider introduced the
method in *A new technique for measuring extragalactic distances* (1988).
They pointed out that the pixel-to-pixel fluctuations in the image of a
galaxy whose stars are not resolved depend on the galaxy's distance, and
can therefore be used to measure it.

**First applications (1990–1994).** Tonry, Ajhar & Luppino (1990)
observed fluctuations in Virgo cluster galaxies. Tonry (1991) presented
SBF as "a bridge from M31 to the Hubble constant". Ajhar & Tonry (1994)
measured fluctuations in globular clusters to connect $\bar M$ to stellar
populations.

**The I-band SBF Survey (1997–2001).** Tonry, Blakeslee, Ajhar and
Dressler carried out the ground-based *SBF Survey of Galaxy Distances*
(Tonry et al. 1997, 2000, 2001). The final catalogue has 300 galaxies
with a median distance-modulus error of 0.22 mag. It was used for the
Hubble constant and for the flows of galaxies in the local universe.

**Infrared SBF (1996–2003).** Joseph Jensen, with Tonry and Luppino,
developed the method in the near-infrared. There the fluctuations are
brighter relative to the galaxy: Virgo ellipticals (Jensen, Luppino &
Tonry 1996), the IR SBF method (Jensen, Tonry & Luppino 1998), the Hydra
and Coma clusters (1999), and, with HST/NICMOS, an IR SBF Hubble constant
(Jensen et al. 2001) and an IR calibration from 65 galaxies that also
probes their stellar populations (Jensen et al. 2003).

**Stellar populations (2001).** Blakeslee, Vazdekis & Ajhar (2001)
compared new observations with stellar population models of SBF,
connecting $\bar M$ to age and metallicity.

**HST/ACS (2005–2010).** John Blakeslee and collaborators brought SBF to
the Advanced Camera for Surveys. They set out the reduction procedures
and calibration (Mei et al. 2005a, 2005b), mapped the 3-D structure of the
Virgo cluster (Mei et al. 2007), measured the Fornax–Virgo relative
distance precisely (Blakeslee et al. 2009), and calibrated the F814W
bandpass (Blakeslee et al. 2010).

**HST/WFC3-IR and the Hubble constant (2015–2021).** Jensen, Blakeslee
and collaborators calibrated IR SBF for WFC3/IR, with advice on
measurement (Jensen et al. 2015). They then measured distances to 63
massive early-type galaxies out to 100 Mpc, with a median uncertainty of
3.9% (Jensen et al. 2021). From these distances they derived
$H_0 = 73.3 \pm 0.7 \pm 2.4$ km/s/Mpc (Blakeslee et al. 2021).

**Ground-based wide-field surveys (2018).** Cantiello, Blakeslee and the
Next Generation Virgo Cluster Survey measured and calibrated SBF
distances for bright galaxies in Virgo and beyond (Cantiello et al.
2018).

**Today.** SBF is reviewed by Cantiello & Blakeslee (2023, 2024). They
describe an approved JWST program to tie the SBF zeropoint to
tip-of-the-red-giant-branch distances.

!!! note "ELLIPROF history is separate"
    ELLIPROF was originally developed by John Tonry as part of MONSTA, an
    image-analysis package. This page is the history of the SBF
    *method*. It does not imply that any particular study above used
    ELLIPROF. See [About and credits](../about.md) for ELLIPROF's own
    history.
